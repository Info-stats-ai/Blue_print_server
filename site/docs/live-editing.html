Syncing and merging

Files move between you and your team when they're saved. Changes to different parts of a file merge on their own, and every version is kept, so you don't need git to work together.

Choose how files move

Use the switch at the top of Files in Team activity. You can change it at any time.

ModeYour filesTeammates' files
AutoSent a moment after you save them.Arrive on their own and merge with your work.
ManualSent only when you press sync on a file, or Sync all.Wait for you, marked to review.

In Manual, teammates' AIs work without what you haven't sent yet. That's the trade for keeping your work to yourself until it's ready.

How merging works

When a teammate's version of a file arrives and you've changed it too:

  • Different parts changed: both sets of changes are kept.
  • Both added something in the same place, such as two new functions: both are kept, and both of you end up with the same file.
  • The same lines changed two ways: nothing is merged. The file is marked conflict for everyone in the session until someone picks a version, merges them, or asks their AI to.

A file in conflict or waiting for review is never sent on its own, so no one's work is overwritten without a choice.

Go back to an earlier version

Every version that reaches the team is kept, with who made it and when. In Files, select the clock on a file, then View or Restore. A restored version becomes your newest change. Git keeps working as usual if your project uses it.

What your AIs learn

When a teammate's file reaches you, your AIs are told who sent it, what changed and what it defines, so they use the real code instead of guessing. A version still waiting for your review is readable by them too, without touching your copy.

Live typing (experimental)

With Auto sync, you can also turn on Live typing in open files from the gear in Files. Edits then appear for each other keystroke by keystroke. It's off by default while we finish testing it.

Files that are never sent

Files that usually hold credentials (.env, .ssh, .aws, keys and certificates), your private paths, and any file that looks like it contains a key or password stay on your machine. Keys pasted into chat are masked. This catches common cases, not every secret, so keep secrets out of your code.
